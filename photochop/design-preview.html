A · Paper

  • Feel: warm off-white workspace, white panels, soft shadows, one burnt-orange accent. Reads like a well-made desktop app, not a dashboard.
  • Why it is easier: the canvas is the darkest thing on screen, so the eye lands on the work. Text is 13px everywhere, no uppercase micro-labels, no gradients or grid noise in the chrome.
  • Risk: photographers often prefer a neutral dark surround for colour judgement. Mitigation: the canvas surround has its own setting (light / mid / dark) independent of the theme, and a dark theme ships too.

B · Graphite

  • Feel: neutral dark grey (not navy), hairline borders, crisp 6px corners, electric-blue accent. The "serious tool" option.
  • Why it is easier: same simplified layout as A, with higher contrast text than OpenShop (grey-on-navy was the biggest readability problem). Neutral grey surround is colour-accurate for photo work.
  • Risk: the safest and most familiar, so the least distinctive. Mitigation: the layer-effects stack and command search still make it feel new.

C · Focus

  • Feel: canvas fills the window; everything else floats on frosted panels (tool dock bottom-centre, layers card on the right, search pill on top). Lime accent.
  • Why it is easier: the same layout on desktop, tablet and phone, so nothing has to be relearned. The tool dock sits where thumbs are. Panels can be hidden with one key (Tab) and the picture is all that remains.
  • Risk: floating panels cover artwork on small desktops, and frosted glass costs GPU on weak laptops. Mitigation: panels snap to the edge and become opaque below 1200px wide.

Shared in every direction (this is the actual redesign)

  • One tool rail, 13 groups instead of 20 slots with hidden flyouts. Hovering shows the name and shortcut; a small corner mark means the group opens into related tools (right-click or long-press).
  • A context bar that changes with the tool. Nothing else in the chrome changes, so the layout never jumps.
  • One right panel: Layers on top, Inspector below. The Inspector shows whatever is selected (layer, text, shape, effect). Colour, History, Navigator and Info are popovers and status-bar items, not permanent panels. OpenShop had nine tabs across three stacked panels.
  • Adjustments and filters are live effects on a layer (the "fx" chips), toggled, reordered and re-edited at any time. OpenShop applies most filters destructively through a dialog.
  • Search is the second way to do everything. Ctrl K finds tools, adjustments, filters, export formats and recent files. No seven-menu bar to memorise.
  • A floating toolbar near the selection for the three or four most likely next actions (text: bold, italic, align, colour, effects).
  • Status bar tells the truth: "Saved on this device" with a timestamp and a live network-request counter, so the privacy promise is visible, not a slogan.
  • Phone mode (toggle above): bottom tool dock, inspector as a draggable sheet, 44px targets, two-finger tap to undo.
