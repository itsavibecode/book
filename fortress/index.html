How we label claims

Two separate ratings. The stamp says how well a claim is supported. The trust blocks say how reliable an outlet is in general. The automated wire never changes a label: a script cannot know if something is true.

Labels

LabelWhen we use it
FactA primary source exists: an official release, transcript, government page, court or budget document.
ConfirmedTwo or more independent outlets rated 4 or higher report it with consistent details.
ReportedOne outlet rated 3 or higher reports it. No second source yet.
RumorOnly social posts, forums, or blogs rated 1 or 2.
SpeculationA theory about why, not a claim of what happened. Always tagged with its theory.
DebunkedAn official denial or a fact-checker contradicts it.

Flags next to a stamp: DEVELOPING means the claim is dated or changed in the last 48 hours. UPDATED means its label or details changed in the last 7 days, with a one-line note. CORRECTION means we downgraded a label we got wrong; the old label stays in the claim's history. SIMPSONS marks a claim people tie to a Simpsons episode: a tag, never a status. DATE marks a scheduled event, with a countdown when it is less than 30 days away.

Outlet trust, 1 to 5

TrustWho lands here
5Primary documents and official statements (Department of War, gov.uk, European Commission, FEMA, FCC) and wire services.
4Papers of record, major broadcasters and specialist outlets with corrections policies, and fact-checkers that publish their sources.
3Aggregating magazines and local TV. Also an outlet that is the only source for its own claim.
2Tabloids, opinion sites, partisan outlets and re-reporters.
1Anonymous blogs, social posts on Facebook, Instagram, X or YouTube, Reddit threads, Telegram.

Rating an outlet is not rating the claim. A trust-5 outlet can carry a REPORTED claim that has only one source, and a trust-1 post can point at a FACT once we find the primary source. Each outlet's one-line reason is in the claim's sources panel.

Is the starter kit your advice? No. Every item in it comes from the official checklists it links (Ready.gov and the UK's Be Ready campaign), with their quantities in their wording. We add nothing of our own, and the Amazon buttons only open a search.
